Grammar practicePráctica de gramática

Present perfect vs past simplePresente perfecto vs pasado simple

This week you will practice choosing between the present perfect and the past simple to describe the history, treatment, and progress of patients with herpes zoster (shingles). Esta semana practicarás cómo elegir entre el presente perfecto y el pasado simple para describir la historia, el tratamiento y la evolución de pacientes con herpes zóster (culebrilla).

Choosing the right tense tells the team whether something is finished or still relevant now, which is essential for accurate documentation, safe medication timing, and clear handovers. Elegir el tiempo verbal correcto le dice al equipo si algo ya terminó o si sigue siendo relevante ahora, lo cual es esencial para una documentación precisa, horarios seguros de medicación y entregas de turno claras.

Present perfect (have/has + past participle) for experiences and situations relevant now, often with ever, never, already, yet, just, for, or since
She has had shingles for five days. Have you ever had chickenpox?
Past simple for finished actions at a specific past time, such as yesterday, at 10 a.m., or in 2019
The rash appeared on Tuesday. He took his first dose at 10 a.m.
Negatives and questions: hasn't/haven't + past participle compared with didn't + base verb
The daughter hasn't visited yet. The patient didn't sleep well last night. When did the pain start?
Key contrast: never use a specific past time with the present perfect
I have seen the patient. I saw the patient at 9 a.m. (not "I have seen the patient at 9 a.m.")
